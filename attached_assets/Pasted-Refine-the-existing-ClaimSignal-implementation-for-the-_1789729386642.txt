Refine the existing ClaimSignal implementation for the CLM-2205 / Mei Chen scenario.

Do not change the overall visual design, Supabase schema, routing, risk values, working intervention workflow, dashboard filters, or test setup.

The goal of this refinement is to make one product insight immediately obvious:

A delayed claim is not automatically a risky claim.

ClaimSignal should use context to distinguish genuine operational escalation from acceptable or customer-requested delay.

Do not add new features.
Do not redesign the page.
Focus on wording, hierarchy, clarity, and product storytelling.

==================================================
1. CLM-2205 — MEI CHEN
==================================================

This is an important demo scenario.

Keep the existing:

- Claim ID: CLM-2205
- Customer: Mei Chen
- Claim type: Travel
- Previous risk: 65
- Current risk: 32
- Risk level: Low
- Direction: Decreased
- Main signal: Delay accepted by customer

Do not change these values.

The intended product story is:

The claim initially appeared risky because of inactivity and delay.

However, the claim record shows that the customer explicitly accepted or requested the delay while travelling.

That context changes the interpretation of inactivity.

ClaimSignal should therefore reduce apparent escalation risk instead of blindly treating all delays as dangerous.

==================================================
2. MAKE THE RISK DECREASE THE HERO
==================================================

Make the following visually obvious:

Risk 65 → 32

Direction:

Decreased

This contrast is the core proof point.

The viewer should be able to understand within a few seconds that the system did not simply accumulate risk signals.

It reconsidered the meaning of the delay based on context.

==================================================

==================================================

Keep the existing What Changed? structure.

“Customer context reduced apparent risk”

Supporting text:

“The claim remains delayed, but the customer explicitly requested the pause while travelling.”

Keep:

Risk 65 → 32 · decreased

Use a calmer green/neutral treatment rather than red or warning styling where appropriate.

Do not imply that all risk has disappeared.

==================================================
4. ADD A CONTEXT CHECK
==================================================

Create or refine a context card with the headline:

“Delay is expected and customer-approved”

Supporting copy:

“The operational timeline shows inactivity, but the customer explicitly accepted the delay until returning from travel. In this context, inactivity is not currently a strong escalation signal.”

Important:

Do not say:

- “There is no risk”
- “The claim is safe”
- “The customer will not complain”

Use bounded language such as:

- lower apparent risk
- reduced escalation concern
- context changes the meaning of inactivity
- continue monitoring

==================================================
11. DEMO SUCCESS CRITERIA
==================================================

The refinement is successful if a viewer can understand the Mei Chen scenario in approximately 10 seconds:

1. The claim previously looked risky at 65.
2. Current risk is 32.
3. The delay still exists.
4. The customer explicitly requested or accepted the delay.
5. That context changes the meaning of inactivity.
6. ClaimSignal reduces apparent escalation risk.
7. The system does not recommend unnecessary intervention.
8. Human review remains required.

The final product insight should feel obvious:

“ClaimSignal does not treat every delay as risk. Context can reduce false positives.”
